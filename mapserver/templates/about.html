About

MAPIYA is an efficient tool for the identification of molecular contacts in multi-component systems composed of various types of biomolecules, including proteins, nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), peptides, and other small ligands.

MAPIYA is composed of four analytical modules:
• chord diagram, shows the network of interacting componensts within the biological system
• heatmap chart, shows the residue-resolution map of intra- or intermolecular contacts
• molstar visualizer, shows the three-dimensional struture of the biological complex
• download section, shows the results as a raw data converted to user-friendly format.

Analysis of contact interactions

Proteins can interact multivalently with a myriad of various bimolecules, including other proteins and nucleic acids.
The strength and physicochemical character of intermolecular contacts between these complex systems is a driving force of signaling or regulatory processes, while the identification of these contacts in the available 3D structures is essential for biomedical and biotechnological applications.


The MapView of the MAPIYA web server consists of interactive heatmap chart. The graph has three available display modes:
• DM, Distance Map, where the points are colored with a continous color scale according to the distance between corresponding residue pairs,
• CM, Contact Map, where the points are colored with a discrete (or smooth) color scale according to the identified spatial contacts,
• CM | DM, where the chart is split into two triangles (this mode is available only for INTRA molecular maps).
The display mode can be selected within Basic options section of settings. There you can also change on-the-fly the distance cutoff for contacts.
Multiple Color scales are available for user convenience. Discrete (binary) color scales for CM mode can be easily smoothed to continuous scales, making it easy to distinguish the strength of contacts by distance of interacting residues.
Using Filter contacts options, you can cross-select contacts by properties or screen-out them by sequential neighborhood.
Specifically, the Interaction Filter selects contacts by various physicochemical properties of interacting residues. The available options include:
• hydropathy: hydrophobic, hydrophilic, amphipatic
• electrostatics: ion-ion, ion-dipole, dipole-dipole
• π-electron stacking: π-π, cation-π, anion-π, dipole-π
• hydrogen bonds (calculated using EDHB)
Additionally, the intra-Contact Filter removes local contacts between residues neighboring along the sequence. As a result, the points along diagonal of intramolecular (internal) contact map are dropped. The filter takes an integer n, which defines the number of amino acids (i, i+1, ..., i+n) along the sequence, for which contacts are excluded.

The maps are customizable in multiple ways.
Using Display 1D data options, you can add various one-dimensional features along the objects' sequence. The residue-resolution bar-chart will be displayed on the right side parallel to the Y, or on the top side parallel to the X axis. Note, that some contacts can be multivalent (e.g., have both charge and π-electrons) thus such additional sequence- and structure-dependent data facilitate the comprehensive analysis of interactions contributions.
The available options include:
• various physicochemical properties,
• amino acid composition,
• secondary structure, calculated using STRIDE,
• solvent accessibility, calculated using STRIDE,
• Shannon enthropy, calculated on-the-fly,
• hydrogen bond donor/acceptor,
• and more.

Example case study

The selected example shows the MAPIYA results for a Human DNA polymerase beta complexed with gapped DNA (PDB: 1BPY).
The MapView presents the intermolecular contact map (CM mode) between protein chain A (protein-A, on the X-axis) and DNA strand T (nucleic-T, on the Y-axis). Gray points on the heatplot (left panel) are contacts identified below the distance cutoff of 8.0 A. From these, contacts stabilized by attractive electrostatic interactions (A, marked in red-purple palette) were selected using an Interaction Filter. More specifically, we want to see which contacts are bound by the salt bridge. Therefore, we selected 'charged' from the options available for protein-A (feature for the X-axis) in the Display 1D data section of settings. The bar-chart displayed on the top of the Contact Map shows the pattern of positively (light blue) and negatively (dark blue) charged amino acids along the sequence of protein-A. Since nucleic acid is strongly negatively charged, salt bridges can be formed with positively charged amino acids such as Arg, Lys, and His. For a better view, we zoomed the interactive graph to the 225-305 fragment along the protein-A sequence. We found an isolated contact beetween ARG:258 and DG:9 (marked with black dot).
Clicking on a selected point invokes two actions:
• displays an interactive label with details describing the contact (residues identifiers, distance, possible interaction forces),
• automatically zooms the Mol* Viewer window (right panel) on the residues that are in contact, revealing the atomic details of the interaction.
